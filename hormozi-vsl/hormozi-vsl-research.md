# Hormozi on VSLs: research notes

Compiled 2026-10-27 from his videos (transcripts pulled with yt-dlp), his books, and second-hand write-ups. Everything below is paraphrased.

**Tags:**
- **[H]** heard him say it in a transcript.
- **[B]** from his books ($100M Offers / Leads / Money Models), from summaries.
- **[S]** someone else relaying his teaching.
- **[I]** inference, not his words.

Source numbers refer to the list at the end.

## 1. Structures

### Local / pre-appointment VSL [H: S1, S2; S12 agrees]

This is the one in `reference-breakdown.md`:

1. **Intro:** Promise, Pain, Proof, Plan, Pic. "Pic" is a visual road map. It is written on the flip chart; the audio is unclear at that point.
2. **Unique thing:** your named mechanism. He says "1 to 3 minutes", then "90 seconds tops". Aim for 60–90 s and treat 3 min as the ceiling.
3. **Objections:** every big one, each through the belief-breaking formula: what they think → why it's wrong → what's right → proof.
4. **CTA:** only the CTA changes between uses. Before the visit it's "expect our guy"; after the visit it's the next step.

### Long-form, no-call / low-ticket VSL [S: S3; the close matches his own spoken close in S4]

His breakdown of his Skool VSL, relayed by a creator:

1. **Call-out and pain**, at the viewer's awareness level.
2. **Bridge story:** backstory, the bad situation, the change or mechanism, the obstacle, the outcome.
3. **Credibility:** why listen to you.
4. **Offer, casually.** High level, with analogies.
5. **FAQ:** the questions prospects ask before buying. Each gets a simple answer or analogy.
6. **Proof:** at least 5 case stories, each bad → change → outcome. Pick each one to defuse a hidden objection. Then a run of stats ("why I'm so confident").
7. **Price anchor:** "others charge $X…"
8. **Concerns:** each one answered with a case study and a policy.
9. **Next steps:** exactly what happens after the click.
10. **Best-case / worst-case close:** the worst case is still a win.
11. **Guarantee, real scarcity or urgency, and fast-action bonuses.**

Reported claim: most of the result comes from the hook, the FAQs and the proof. Not confirmed in a primary source.

### High-ticket [H: S5]

Put one or more VSLs in the process so prospects arrive at the first call already educated. Pair this with BANT qualification. Deep objection handling stays on the call.

### Group funnels [H: S6]

The VSL is split into five videos:

1. The opportunity and your story.
2. to 4. Three belief-breaking stories, about the vehicle, about other people, and about the viewer.
5. The CTA, with a little stack and sizzle.

A VSL on a landing page is "good friction": it lowers lead volume but raises lead quality.

### Pitching the process [H: S7]

Group your steps into three pillars. The right level of detail depends on the offer:

- **DIY or done-with-you:** keep it simple.
- **Done-for-you:** show all the steps, so they believe it works but don't want to do it themselves.

Avoid long feature stacks.

## 2. Hooks

**Why they matter**
- The hook is everything. Most viewers are lost in the first 3–5 seconds [H: S8].
- A great headline can beat the control several times over; most other tests move results 20–50% [H: S9].

**How to write one** [H: S9]
- Call out who it's for, and who it's *not* for. The disqualifier is the stronger of the two.
- Write to one person.
- Describe specific moments, not categories.
- Match the hook to their awareness:
  - unaware → curiosity;
  - problem-aware → a yes-question about the problem;
  - solution-aware → "tried X and it didn't work?";
  - most aware → just make the offer.
- A favourite formula: "How to [good thing] without [bad thing], even if [biggest fear]."

**Reusing hooks** [H: S8]
- About 80% of the time he reuses past winning hooks with a new background or props; about 20% goes to new ones.
- Take the first 3 seconds of your best ads and splice them onto other bodies [H: S6].

**Ad and message structure**
- Ads: call-out → value → CTA. Content: Hook → Retain → Reward [B, via S10].
- **Rule of One:** one big idea, easy to understand, easy to believe, interesting. Every part should serve it [H: S13].

## 3. Offer framing

**Value equation [B]**
- Raise the dream outcome and the perceived likelihood.
- Cut the time delay and the effort.
- Present every component as moving them away from bad and toward good, and say what it does for risk, speed and ease [H: S4].

**Stack and bonuses**
- Each bonus kills one objection, and is named and valued [B].
- Fast-action bonuses give people a reason to buy now [S: S3].

**Scarcity and urgency** [H: S9, S14; B]
- Scarcity is quantity; urgency is time.
- Both must be real, and the cutoff must be honoured.
- A service business's capacity limit is a real limit, so say it.

**Reason why** [H: S9, S14]
- Always give a "because", even a small one.

**Guarantees** [B]
- Unconditional, conditional, anti-guarantee, or implied. They can be stacked.
- In a VSL they show up as the "worst case is still good" frame.

**Other tools**
- Naming with MAGIC: Magnetic reason, Avatar, Goal, Interval, Container word [B, via S15].
- Price anchor: say a high number before the real price [S: S3].

## 4. Proof, objections, CTA

**Proof**
- Proof beats promise. Lead with what only you can show [H: S9].
- Implied authority: years in business, total jobs done, review count [H: S9].
- **Damaging admission:** a true flaw, then "but", then the claim. The part after "but" is the part people believe [H: S9].
- Be specific about the pain and more measured about the promise [H: S16].
- Proof should come from people who look like the viewer [S: S3].

**Objections**
- Source them from the questions people ask before buying [S: S3, S12].
- Put the most common objection into a case study's "before" [S: S3].

**CTA** [H: S9, S14]
- Clear, not clever.
- The exact next action, plus a reason to act now.
- Spell out what happens after they act. When that comes true, they trust your next instruction too.
- Always end with a P.S.: a short recap, or who this isn't for. In a VSL that is the end card [I].

## 5. Delivery

- **Third-grade reading level.** Short sentences, small words, big proof. Fancy is friction [H: S9].
- **Show, don't tell.** Describe concrete moments and put them on screen [H: S9].
- **Unboxing frame.** He criticised an all-talking-head sales video. Show each part of the offer on screen while it's being described [H: S4].
- **Road map up front** [H: S1].
- **Tie benefits to status:** who grants it, and over what timeframe [H: S9].
- **Not covered by him:** face vs. faceless, captions and total length [I]. A faceless VSL has no face to trust, so it should lean harder on proof, numbers and implied authority [I].

## 6. Testing

- **One split test per week** [H: S17].
  - Most landing-page tests are the headline or the image above the fold.
  - Test order: headline, then image, then subtext [B, via S10].
- **Keep the body, rotate the hook.** Make many variants of a winner [H: S6; S: S11].
- **Metrics to track:**
  - click-through rate;
  - above-the-fold conversion;
  - lead quality;
  - show rate [H: S6, S9, S17, S19].
- **Show-rate levers** [H: S19]:
  - more open slots;
  - 15-minute booking increments;
  - same-day booking;
  - pulling appointments forward.
- **For a pre-appointment VSL** [I]:
  - compare show rate and first-visit close rate before vs. after adding it;
  - track how many leads watch it, and how far they get.

## 8. From the full YouTube corpus (2026-10-27)

**Corpus:** every video on @AlexHormozi, both videos and streams. 551 of 558 have transcripts, totalling **281.9 hours**. The transcripts themselves aren't included. A keyword search found every mention of VSL, video sales letter, sales letter and sales video: 57 core passages, read in full. All findings below are **[H]**. Links are timestamped.

**Corrections to the sections above:**
- **Length.** He says it outright: "five to seven minutes. Doesn't need to be long." ([3 Small Changes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3yAiVjcImQ4&t=704s), [Confronted a Stranger](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6Fg4VXjRphQ&t=1173s))
- **Five P's order.** He also lists them proof-first: "proof, promise, pain, plan, picture". He calls a VSL the same process as his YouTube videos. ([Easy Mode 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ds_Qp2U5I8U&t=1147s), [$1M Business for a Stranger](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HGZOxBfnF-E&t=693s))
- **Objections.** He wants the FAQs and deal-killers answered *in* the VSL, so the call is for closing. ([Service-Based Businesses](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mRlSb0O5QNU&t=1201s), [lighting caller](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A8p2HCI_23E&t=2039s))
- **Later VSLs.** A renewal or second VSL changes its examples and objections, not just the CTA, and a second VSL is mostly "more proof". ([renewal](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mRlSb0O5QNU&t=1201s), [second VSL](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A8p2HCI_23E&t=2297s))

**New:**

*The opening*
- Beginners meander for 1–2 minutes and lose about 40% of the audience. Open with proof from "someone just like you", then the promise, then the plan. Rewriting only the first 5 minutes of a 3-hour presentation raised close rate by 20%. ([Business Was Stuck](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4GQLJjH9-oA&t=372s))

*What goes in*
- **Dental VSL, worked through:**
  1. promise → pain → "our process fixes that";
  2. 3–4 proofs, one per avatar;
  3. a 4-step road map;
  4. old way vs. our new way, and why the practice exists;
  5. objections through the four moves;
  6. a stack of before/afters and testimonials.

  He calls it "basically a ton of testimonials". ([Required Viewing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uaLNfijnp-8&t=1133s))
- **Second structure:** who / what / where / when / why / how ("what to expect"), then the objections. ([launch stream](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B2QbLzHvKbg&t=20341s))
- **FAQ VSL:** use prospects' exact questions: "A lot of people ask this. This is how we answer it." ([3 Small Changes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3yAiVjcImQ4&t=704s))
- **One offer:** he deletes the downsell line from a pre-call VSL on camera. ([Confronted a Stranger](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6Fg4VXjRphQ&t=1173s))
- **Price:** include it only when demand exceeds sales capacity, as friction; otherwise leave it out. A range is fine. ([Easy Mode](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ds_Qp2U5I8U&t=1443s), [Required Viewing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uaLNfijnp-8&t=1228s))
- **Lead magnet inside the VSL:** tease it early, deliver it about a third of the way in. ([Easy Mode](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ds_Qp2U5I8U&t=1443s))
- **Done-for-you offers:** put all the secrets in; they'll still hire you. ([34.2M Views](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eW04CvHKvMo&t=4314s))

*Where it goes*
- **Funnels:**
  - ad → lead magnet → VSL → BANT → call. He calls the VSL one of the highest-leverage sales aids. ([Easy Mode](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ds_Qp2U5I8U&t=975s))
  - The simplest version is lead ad → VSL → a call-now button under the video. ([Get Rich While You're Young](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DfXy_PzlC0o&t=2741s))
- **One VSL per conversation, and per decision-maker.** He also uses them for recruiting, upsells, and on an iPad in the waiting room. ([Service-Based](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mRlSb0O5QNU&t=1201s), [Required Viewing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uaLNfijnp-8&t=1133s), [Acquisition.com](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hHkdbr6_JJs&t=949s))
- **Not for cold traffic.** A short VSL → book-a-call funnel is warm; for cold traffic use a paid workshop first. ([cold traffic](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_ooj0V4hpgw&t=421s))
- "Basically never sell less with a VSL." The VSL is the *education* lever, next to qualification. ([Required Viewing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uaLNfijnp-8&t=1228s))

*Making sure it works*
- **Check they watched:** confirm it in reminders, use an engagement CTA ("text me your head count"), and open the call with "did you watch the video?" ([lighting](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A8p2HCI_23E&t=2039s), [Service-Based](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mRlSb0O5QNU&t=1201s))
- **Improve it:**
  - Use drop-off analysis: expand the spikes, fix the drop-offs, starting with the opening. If a recording won't convert, deliver it live a few times to tighten it. ([Business Was Stuck](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4GQLJjH9-oA&t=372s))
  - For the book launch he made six VSLs, each with more visuals, more narrative, a smaller downside and a bigger upside. ([26 Harsh Lessons](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YONIUpk-kAs&t=869s))
- **Early failure:** he admits that at first he couldn't get VSLs or webinars to work and went back to phone calls. ([Sell Better than 99%](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JE2_7elAcxM&t=15206s))

**Numbers:**
- **No clean before/after VSL conversion rate from Hormozi himself.**
- Closest, all from callers or forecasts:
  - A lighting company's close rate went from 3.3% of leads over a year to 5 closes from 168 leads in the weeks after adding a VSL (the caller's numbers). ([link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A8p2HCI_23E&t=1913s))
  - He forecast a 50–60% close rate for a railings owner following the full VSL + BANT process. ([link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HGZOxBfnF-E&t=693s))
  - 78% of his own buyers watched about an hour of his content before buying. ([link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ds_Qp2U5I8U&t=1147s))
- **Not covered in the corpus:** face vs. no face, phone vs. studio.
